# Lesson 02 — "I am not a programmer"

*2026-08-22 · Palo Alto AI Research Lab*

This is the most common thing people say to me, and it is aimed at the wrong thing.

You do not need to write code. You need to describe a task well. Those are different skills, and you already have the second one.

## How it actually works

You open the tool. You type a sentence, in your own language, the way you would brief a new hire on their first morning:

> Make me a spending table out of these receipts.

You drop in the photos. A minute later you have the table.

You did not write a line of code. Code happened, somewhere inside, the way an engine happens under the hood of your car. You never see it and you do not need to.

The first step is smaller than it looks: install the program, type "hello". That is the whole first step. After that it is a conversation, and you hold conversations better than most programmers do.

## The same thing, on our own mess

The receipts example is the small version. Here is the full-size one from this lab, this week.

My tasks live as plain text files in a folder. Not a system, a folder: **898 files** at the time of writing, one per task, written by me and by the agents over months. Nobody could tell you what was actually open.

I did not ask for software. I described the outcome:

> Read every task file. Show me one board: what is still open, what is priority zero, and which ones have gone the longest without anyone touching them.

What came back, today at 17:35: **357 open, 16 of them priority zero, 178 priority one**, and the oldest stalled item — 24 days without a move. There is now a board that recomputes itself, and it is the first thing I see when I sit down.

Three things worth noticing about that request:

1. There is no technical word in it. "Read the files, show me a board."
2. It is about **my** operations, not about building an app. Nobody needed a product; I needed to know what I owe.
3. The unpleasant answer — 357 open, 11 rotting — arrived without being softened. That is the point of asking a machine instead of asking yourself.

The distance between "table from receipts" and "board from 898 files" is one evening of practice. It is not a career change.

## What this does not solve

Being able to ask is not being able to check. A machine will happily hand you a confident table built on the wrong half of the folder. That is Lesson 05, and it is the one that decides whether you are still using this in a month.

Today, just get to "hello".

## Do this today

Open the tool. Type one sentence about something dull you own: a folder of invoices, a stack of PDFs, an inbox you have been avoiding. Ask for the result, not the code.
